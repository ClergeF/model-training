MODEL REFERENCE STANDARDMoment Detection Model LogicDefinition and Detection Rules with Model Interface and ExamplesVersion 1.3  |  September 15, 2026Primary counting rule: 1 bounded interaction × 1 beneficiary = 1 Moment.Historical rule: Past tense never disqualifies a Moment. Confirm it when the text gives enough evidence to establish the actual interaction.Required input: The model receives only originalText and source. It extracts people, roles, type, and category from the text.Source of truth for labeling, evaluation, prompting, and production inferencePurpose and ScopeThis document defines exactly what the Moment Detection Model is supposed to detect. It is the canonical reference for annotators, prompt writers, developers, evaluators, and downstream systems. If another instruction conflicts with this document, this document controls until an updated version replaces it.1 Canonical DefinitionDefinition: A Moment is one meaningful, bounded check-in in which one person intentionally reaches out to, checks on, supports, helps, informs, asks something of, follows up with, mentors, gives to, serves, or otherwise meaningfully engages another identifiable person.A Moment represents the interaction as a whole. It is not every sentence, action, topic detail, message, or resource inside that interaction.2 The Unit of DetectionThe model detects a relationship-specific interaction:Core structure: Contributor → meaningful bounded interaction → BeneficiaryContributor: the person whose outreach or check-in is being recorded.Beneficiary: the identifiable person reached, checked on, asked, supported, informed, mentored, served, or otherwise engaged.Bounded interaction: the continuous real-world check-in being described, including all closely related actions within it.3 Required CriteriaA record may be confirmed as a Moment only when the available text and context establish all required criteria:Two people are involved, or the missing identity can be resolved from the original text.The interaction is intentional and directed from one person toward another.The interaction performs a qualifying check-in function, such as care, information exchange, follow-up, support, mentorship, giving, service, or another meaningful form of engagement.The text provides enough evidence to understand what happened. Past tense is allowed; vagueness is the issue, not time.The interaction has a usable boundary, so the model can determine which actions belong to the same occurrence.The beneficiary is identifiable from the original text if an individual Moment record is to be emitted.4 Detection OutcomesThese are model-decision states. They are not required database fields unless the implementation adds them.OutcomeMeaningModel actionCONFIRMEDEnough evidence establishes a qualifying interaction.Emit the Moment record.POTENTIALThe statement suggests a Moment but lacks identity, action detail, boundary, or other required context.Retain for context review; do not silently discard or overclaim.NOT_A_MOMENTThe content is social noise, observation, unrelated activity, or fails the qualifying criteria.Do not emit a Moment record.5 Interaction Boundaries and Counting5 1 One Continuous Interaction with One PersonMultiple actions toward the same beneficiary remain one Moment when they are part of one continuous check-in with one shared purpose or closely connected topic.Example — one Moment: John checks on Sarah about school, discusses her math problem, gives advice, and sends a tutoring resource during the same check-in. Count 1 Moment, not 3.5 2 Multiple BeneficiariesA single group interaction creates one Moment per identifiable beneficiary. Shared time, location, message, or topic does not merge different contributor-to-beneficiary relationships.Example — five Moments: John gives the same college advice to Sarah, Mike, James, Kayla, and Chris together. Emit 5 Moment records: John → Sarah, John → Mike, John → James, John → Kayla, and John → Chris.If the text only says “I helped a group” and the individual people cannot be identified, treat it as POTENTIAL until the recipients can be resolved. Do not invent names or IDs.5 3 Multiple ContributorsWhen several people independently contribute to the same beneficiary, create one record per established contributor-beneficiary relationship. If the text describes a collective effort but does not distinguish individual contributions, preserve the event as POTENTIAL or follow the product’s explicit group-attribution rule. Never fabricate individual credit.5 4 When a New Moment StartsStart a new Moment when the evidence establishes a genuinely new bounded interaction, such as:a later, separate check-in after the earlier interaction ended;a new outreach attempt rather than another action inside the same exchange;a different beneficiary; ora clearly separate occurrence that the speaker distinguishes by time, setting, or purpose.A topic shift alone does not automatically create another Moment. Use the interaction boundary first, then purpose and topic as supporting evidence.6 Historical and Reported MomentsNon-negotiable rule: Historical ≠ non-Moment. Vague ≠ confirmed Moment.The model may detect a Moment from an interaction happening now or from a person describing an interaction that happened earlier. “A minute ago,” “yesterday,” “last week,” or “years ago” does not disqualify the interaction.The deciding question is: Does the full text provide enough evidence to reconstruct a real qualifying interaction between identifiable people?TextDecisionReason“I helped Sarah yesterday.”POTENTIALPast tense is allowed, but “helped” is too vague alone.“I helped Sarah with her homework a minute ago.”POTENTIAL / strong candidateThe activity is clearer, but surrounding detail may still be needed to establish how the check-in occurred.“Sarah was stuck on algebra yesterday, so I sat with her and showed her how to solve the equations.”CONFIRMEDThe people, action, purpose, and historical occurrence are established.“Remember when somebody helped Sarah?”POTENTIAL / insufficientThe contributor and qualifying details are unresolved.“Sarah, do you still need help with your homework?”CONFIRMEDA present follow-up check-in is directly occurring.Use the complete available context, not a single sentence in isolation. A vague sentence may become confirmed when nearby messages identify the people, action, purpose, and boundary.For quoted examples using I or an unnamed speaker, CONFIRMED assumes that originalText identifies the speaker elsewhere. Without that context, return POTENTIAL and request the missing name.7 Contributor and Beneficiary DirectionDirection must reflect the specific check-in being logged. Use the product schema and the evidence in the text; do not assume that the person receiving material benefit is always the contributor or beneficiary.For a direct check-in, the initiator or person reaching out is normally the contributor; the person reached is the beneficiary.For an information request, the person reaching out to ask is normally the contributor for that check-in; the person asked is the beneficiary under the current schema.If the recorded interaction focuses on help, guidance, money, time, resources, or service that was provided, the provider may be the contributor and the receiver the beneficiary.If one exchange contains both a request and a response, keep it as one bounded Moment unless the product explicitly logs each direction separately. Choose the direction that matches the main check-in being recorded.When direction cannot be resolved, return POTENTIAL and state what information is missing. Never guess a name or identity.8 momentType ClassificationmomentType describes how the person checked in. Assign the single best primary type supported by the interaction. Multiple actions in one Moment do not automatically create multiple types or Moments.TypeCanonical meaningWELLNESS_CHECKMaking sure someone is okay.INFO_SHARINGProviding useful information to someone.INFO_REQUESTReaching out to get information from someone.FOLLOW_UPFollowing up on a previous conversation or check-in.SUPPORTOffering emotional or practical support.MENTORSHIPGuidance or mentoring between people.MONETARYProviding financial support.TIMEGiving time.DONATIONDonating resources.SERVICEProviding a service.OTHERAnother qualifying check-in that does not fit the defined types.8 1 Type Selection RulesChoose the most specific type that represents the central purpose of the bounded interaction.Use FOLLOW_UP when reconnecting to a prior check-in is the main function, even if information or support is also present.Use MENTORSHIP for developmental guidance; use INFO_SHARING for mainly factual or resource-based information.Use SUPPORT for emotional or practical help that is not more specifically monetary, time, donation, service, or mentorship.Use OTHER only after the defined options have been considered.9 Category ClassificationCritical distinction: momentType answers “What kind of check-in was this?” Category answers “What broader subject area was this check-in about?”Category is inferred by the model from originalText. It is not supplied as an input and must not be treated as a synonym for momentType. The model should return the category name rather than inventing a category ID.CategoryUse when the subject primarily concernsOutreach NotesGeneral outreach, relationship notes, contact attempts, and check-in context not better represented by a more specific knowledge category.Family & HistoryFamily relationships, personal history, heritage, life background, or historical context.Business & FinanceEmployment, entrepreneurship, money, financial planning, commerce, or professional development.Community & HealthCommunity connection, wellbeing, health, care, safety, or access to support.Faith & SpiritualityFaith, spirituality, worship, religious community, or spiritual guidance.Education & InnovationLearning, school, training, mentorship, technology, ideas, or innovation.9 1 Category Handling RulesDetermine whether the text contains a Moment before assigning its category.Choose the single best primary category from the full originalText.If two categories are plausible, prefer the central purpose rather than every topic mentioned. Preserve ambiguity if no primary category is supported.Never place a momentType value into category or a category value into momentType.Do not invent a categoryId. Any ID mapping belongs to the application after the model responds.9 2 Category ExamplesInteraction subjectPrimary categoryHelping with algebra, school planning, training, or a new technology ideaEducation & InnovationDiscussing work, entrepreneurship, a budget, or financial planningBusiness & FinanceChecking on health, wellbeing, safety, or community supportCommunity & HealthProviding prayer, spiritual encouragement, or faith-based guidanceFaith & SpiritualityRecording family history, heritage, or personal backgroundFamily & HistoryLogging general outreach or contact context with no better subject categoryOutreach Notes10 Model InputThe Moment Detection Model accepts only two input fields. All other information must be extracted or classified from originalText.Input fieldTypeDefinitionoriginalTextStringThe complete text the model must analyze. Confirmed Moments appear inside the moments array. A group interaction produces one separate object per identifiable beneficiary.Top-level fieldTypeDefinitionoriginalTextStringThe input text copied unchanged so the result keeps its evidence.sourceEnumThe supplied MANUAL, API, or IMPORT value copied unchanged.detectionStatusEnumCONFIRMED, POTENTIAL, or NOT_A_MOMENT.momentCountIntegerNumber of confirmed Moment objects returned.momentsObject[]One object per confirmed contributor-to-beneficiary relationship.missingInformationString[]What is still needed for a POTENTIAL Moment; otherwise an empty array.11 1 Fields Inside Each MomentMoment fieldTypeDefinitioncontributorStringName of the person whose outreach or contribution is being recorded.beneficiaryStringName of the person reached or engaged.summaryStringA short factual description of this contributor-beneficiary Moment.momentTypeEnumThe single best approved check-in type.categoryStringThe single best category inferred from originalText.themesString[]Optional supported themes; omit or return an empty array when uncertain.toneStringOptional emotional tone supported by the text.11 2 Output Integrity RulesExtract names from originalText. Do not require IDs and do not invent missing names.Copy originalText and source unchanged at the top level.summary must compress the evidence without adding facts.Themes and tone are optional enrichments and cannot turn a non-Moment into a Moment.One group check-in with five identified beneficiaries requires five separate objects in moments and momentCount must equal 5.IDs, sharing settings, approval status, and category-ID mapping belong to the application after model inference.12 Deterministic Detection ProcedureRead originalText completely and retain the supplied source value.Identify every described or currently occurring person-to-person interaction, including historical ones.For each candidate, identify the contributor, beneficiary, qualifying action, purpose, time reference, and interaction boundary.Decide CONFIRMED, POTENTIAL, or NOT_A_MOMENT using the required criteria. Do not reject an interaction merely because it is historical.Merge actions that belong to one continuous bounded interaction with the same beneficiary.Split a group interaction into one record per identifiable beneficiary. Split separate occurrences when the evidence establishes a new boundary.Assign the contributor-beneficiary direction supported by the main check-in. Preserve ambiguity instead of guessing.Assign one primary momentType.Infer one primary category from originalText.Populate only supported output fields, copy originalText and source unchanged, and run the final acceptance test before emitting records.13 Examples and Edge CasesScenarioCountDecisionWhyFrancky asks Masumi for mentorship advice, explains the problem, asks questions, and receives suggestions in one conversation.1CONFIRMEDOne bounded mentorship interaction.John checks on Sarah, advises her about math, and sends a tutoring link during the same exchange.1CONFIRMEDSeveral actions, one beneficiary and one boundary.John gives the same scholarship information to five named people together.5CONFIRMEDOne record per identifiable beneficiary.“I helped Sarah yesterday.”0 yetPOTENTIALHistorical is allowed; ora vague past-tense claim when the available context does not establish what actually happened.15 Final Acceptance TestBefore emitting each Moment, the model must be able to answer yes or resolve the required value for all of the following:Is the interaction occurring now or presented as an interaction that actually occurred between people?Is the interaction intentional, meaningful, and directed toward another person?Are the contributor and beneficiary known or resolvable without guessing?Is there enough detail to establish what happened, regardless of when it happened?Does this record represent one bounded interaction rather than one sub-action?Has the model created a separate record for each identifiable beneficiary?Is the chosen momentType the best primary type?Has category been inferred from originalText and kept separate from momentType?Are all populated fields supported by originalText and the supplied source value?Has the model avoided duplicates and fabricated details?16 Non Negotiable RulesRule: 1 bounded interaction × 1 beneficiary = 1 Moment.Rule: Multiple related actions toward the same beneficiary inside one continuous interaction remain 1 Moment.Rule: One interaction with five identifiable beneficiaries produces 5 Moment records.Rule: Historical interactions can be valid Moments. Time does not disqualify them; insufficient evidence does.Rule: “I helped Sarah yesterday” is POTENTIAL, not automatically a Moment and not automatically a non-Moment.Rule: The only inputs are originalText and source.Rule: Category is inferred from originalText and remains separate from momentType.Rule: The model must use full context, preserve ambiguity, and never invent people, IDs, events, categories, or details.Version NoteVersion 1.3 defines the production interface as originalText plus source only. The model now extracts people and roles, infers category, returns names instead of villager IDs, and leaves IDs, sharing settings, status, and category-ID mapping to the application. It preserves the Version 1.2 historical-interaction correction.Implementation Handoff ContractAny model, prompt, labeling guide, or evaluation set built from this standard must preserve the following behaviors:Use the complete originalText before deciding that a historical statement is too vague.Keep confirmed, potential, and non-Moment outcomes distinct during labeling and evaluation.Test interaction boundaries, group beneficiary counts, direction, momentType, and category as separate behaviors.Version any future rule change and update examples, procedures, and hard rules together so the document never contradicts itself.17 Five Model Input and Output ExamplesEvery example uses the same two-field input.
